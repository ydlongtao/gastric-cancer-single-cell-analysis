Key Conclusions

The blind annotation performs well at the broad-lineage level. For original cells, broad-lineage ARI is 0.898 and NMI is 0.828. For simulated cells, broad-lineage ARI is 0.999 and NMI is 0.996. This indicates that the 200-gene merged object recovers major T/NK, B/Plasma, Myeloid/TAM, and stromal structures.

Fine cell-type agreement is much lower: original fine-celltype ARI is 0.120, and simulated reference-label ARI is 0.310. Therefore, the cluster calls should be treated as broad-lineage annotations rather than final CD4/CD8/TAM/plasma substate calls.

Cluster Annotation Summary Overview

The merged object contains 19 clusters. The dominant compartments are T cell, Myeloid/TAM, NK cell, Plasma cell, and B cell, with smaller endothelial and fibroblast/pericyte populations. Most clusters have concordant marker-panel and SingleR HPCA evidence and are marked high confidence; two clusters are medium confidence because of reference conflict or unstable reference mapping.

Clusters Requiring Cautious Interpretation

Cluster 15: Myeloid/TAM versus endothelial conflict

Cluster 15 is assigned as Myeloid/TAM by marker scoring, but SingleR points to an endothelial label and the marker-score margin is small. It may reflect myeloid/endothelial signal mixing, a low-information cluster, or reference drift from the 200-gene restriction. It should be reviewed in a larger-gene simulation or full-gene original object.

Cluster 18: plasma-cell markers are clear, but HPCA mapping is abnormal

Cluster 18 contains JCHAIN, MZB1, and DERL3, supporting a plasma-cell interpretation. However, SingleR returns GMP, indicating unstable HPCA annotation on the 200-gene subset. Use this as a plasma-cell tendency and avoid finer subtyping.

Cluster 17: original-cell-enriched inflammatory myeloid cluster

Cluster 17 is supported by S100A8, FCN1, S100A9, and LYZ, and SingleR maps it to CD14+ monocytes. Its original-cell fraction reaches 96.3%, suggesting a real-data inflammatory monocyte/myeloid state that is under-represented in the simulation.
